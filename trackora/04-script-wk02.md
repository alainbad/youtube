## THE PROBLEM (0:20–1:30)

Most first-time importers get one number from their supplier and one from their forwarder, and they add them up.
Then customs sends a bill.

`[B-roll: container port, customs inspection]`

The bill isn't random. Almost every country uses the same three-step formula, and once you know it, nothing surprises you.
The problem is nobody tells you the formula. Your supplier doesn't care, and your forwarder assumes you already know.

And the mistake costs more than the duty itself. If you priced your product on $9,800, your margin just shrank by
12% before you've sold a single item.

## THE SOLUTION — THE 3-STEP FORMULA (1:30–4:30)

### Step 1: Find your CIF value

`[on-screen: C + I + F]`

Customs doesn't charge duty on what you paid the supplier. In most countries, they charge it on **CIF**:
**C**ost of the goods, plus **I**nsurance, plus **F**reight to the port.

Our example:
- Goods: $8,000
- Freight: $1,800
- Insurance: $200

`[on-screen: $8,000 + $200 + $1,800 = $10,000 CIF]`

CIF = **$10,000**. That's the number everything else is calculated on. Notice that you pay duty on your *shipping cost* too.
That's the first thing that catches people out.

### Step 2: Apply the duty rate

The duty rate depends on two things: the country you're importing into and what the product is.
Officially, it's set by the product's HS code, a 6-to-10-digit classification number.

`[on-screen: "HS code" with example 9403 — furniture]`

For furniture into the UAE, the standard GCC rate is 5%.
5% of $10,000 = **$500 duty**.

### Step 3: Apply VAT on top of the duty

This is the step almost everyone misses. VAT isn't charged on the CIF value. It's charged on **CIF plus duty**.
So you pay tax on the tax.

`[on-screen: ($10,000 + $500) × 5% = $525]`

UAE VAT is 5%, so 5% of $10,500 = **$525**.

### Total

`[on-screen: stacked bar — $10,000 CIF | $500 duty | $525 VAT = $11,025]`

$10,000 + $500 + $525 = **$11,025** landed at the border.

## 3 KEY TAKEAWAYS (4:30–5:45)

`[on-screen list, one at a time]`

1. **Duty is charged on CIF, not on what you paid the supplier.** Your freight cost gets taxed too.
2. **VAT is charged on CIF plus duty.** Tax on tax. Budget for it.
3. **Price your product on landed cost, not purchase price.** In our example, that's a 12.5% difference. On thin margins,
   that's the difference between profit and loss.
